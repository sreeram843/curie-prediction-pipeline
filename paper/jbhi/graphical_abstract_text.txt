Severity scores computed from electronic health records can silently treat unmeasured values as normal and can flood clinicians with repeated alerts. We reconstructed the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment score with explicit missing-data states in two intensive care databases and evaluated a versioned alert-governance layer once on a locked MIMIC-IV test set of 14,407 stays. Governance preserved 98.5% loose-window detection while emitting 7.7% of naive interruptions, but only 54.4% of positive stays were detected at least 2 hours before onset, and interruptive precision was 6.1%.
