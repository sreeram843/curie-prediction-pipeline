\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please consider the enclosed manuscript entitled

\begin{center}\itshape
Governing Interruptions After Deterministic Deterioration Scoring:\\
A Retrospective Evaluation on PhysioNet Challenge 2019
\end{center}

for publication as a \textbf{Research and Applications} article in \textit{JAMIA Open}.

\textbf{Significance.}
Most retrospective deterioration studies treat a positive score as the alert.
We instead evaluate the interruption decision as a separable, versioned policy
that runs after a deterministic score and before a page is emitted. Using the
public PhysioNet/Computing in Cardiology Challenge 2019 dataset, we tuned a
dual-lane governance policy on setA, froze it with a content hash, and
evaluated setB once. The governed path matched threshold-only partial-SOFA
detection (79.5\%, 95\% CI 77.0--81.8) while the interruptive lane emitted
13.2\% as many alerts. Normalized Challenge utility was $-0.111$, $-0.053$,
and $0.041$ for threshold-only, governed, and interruptive streams. Against
hourly SIRS, incomplete NEWS2, and partial qSOFA on the same window, and
through drop-one mechanism ablations, we
localize the burden reduction to a 90-minute refractory interval and a page
gate. We believe this mechanism-level, fully reproducible treatment of alert
governance fits \textit{JAMIA Open}'s readership in clinical decision support
and alert fatigue.
