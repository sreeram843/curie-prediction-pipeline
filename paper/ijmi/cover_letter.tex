Please consider the enclosed manuscript entitled

\begin{center}\itshape
Governing Interruptions After Deterministic Deterioration Scoring:\\
A Retrospective Evaluation on PhysioNet Challenge 2019
\end{center}

for publication as an \textbf{Original research article} in the
\textit{International Journal of Medical Informatics}.

\textbf{Significance for IJMI.}
Clinical decision support research often collapses scoring and clinician
interruption into one event. We treat interruption policy as a separable,
versioned governance layer after a deterministic partial-SOFA score. On the
public PhysioNet/Computing in Cardiology Challenge 2019 resource we selected a
dual-lane policy on setA, froze it with a content hash, and evaluated setB once.
Governed detection matched threshold-only sensitivity (79.5\%, 95\% CI
77.0--81.8) while the interruptive lane emitted 13.2\% as many alerts.
Ablations localize the burden reduction to a 90-minute refractory interval and a
page gate. The contribution is methodological and operational: reproducible
alert-governance evaluation for CDS design, not a claim of clinical outcome
improvement.

\textbf{Scope note.}
This is a retrospective offline evaluation of alert routing against a proxy
label on a public challenge holdout. It does not report prospective deployment
or patient outcomes. A brief Clinical Translation Roadmap is included in the
submission package to clarify the intended path from frozen offline evaluation
to prospective CDS governance studies.
